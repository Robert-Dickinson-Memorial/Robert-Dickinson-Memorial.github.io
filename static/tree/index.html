About this tribute and planting locations

Robert grew up in Minnesota. A memorial tree in the Chippewa National Forest honors that connection while helping restore a landscape of pine, spruce, cedar, lakes, and headwater streams.

Reforestation projects in the Chippewa restore native trees, strengthen wildlife habitat, and improve the forest’s resilience to wind damage, insects, disease, and a changing climate.

Why Chippewa comes first

Minnesota was Robert’s childhood home, so Chippewa National Forest remains the anchor of this living tribute. Chippewa National Forest is the first choice; Minnesota forests provide a simpler alternative for smaller gifts and international contributors.

Questions about tree counts and payments

Does the memorial website handle my payment?

No. Payment takes place entirely on the selected provider’s website. The memorial stores only the contribution record you report afterward and never receives your card or banking credentials.

About the landscape images

The landscape artwork evokes the regions connected to Robert’s life. It does not depict or guarantee a specific planting site.

How are tree totals and locations counted?

After successful payment, report the exact tree quantity shown by the provider. We record both that quantity and the geographic level the provider actually guarantees: exact forest/project, state/region, or where needed most.

What if the provider does not state an exact number of trees?

The gift is preserved as a restoration contribution but is not converted into an estimated number of trees.

What if the provider I chose will not accept my international payment?

Use the universal One Tree Planted route or another accessible exact-tree option. The trees still count in Robert’s lifetime total; they are simply attributed to the location level the provider can honestly support.
