Featured tribute · Minnesota

Chippewa National Forest

Robert grew up in Minnesota. A memorial tree in the Chippewa National Forest honors that connection while helping restore a landscape of pine, spruce, cedar, lakes, and headwater streams.

Reforestation projects in the Chippewa restore native trees, strengthen wildlife habitat, and improve the forest’s resilience to wind damage, insects, disease, and a changing climate.

Dedicate trees through Arbor Day Foundation ↗ Plant a smaller grove through A Living Tribute ↗

Arbor Day Foundation: choose an exact tree quantity and select Chippewa National Forest. Its direct commemorative checkout currently has a 10-tree minimum for custom quantities.

A Living Tribute: useful for smaller gifts; select Minnesota → Chippewa National Forest and the number of trees. Planting preferences remain subject to the provider’s active project availability.

Official Forest Service fallback: USDA Plant-A-Tree ↗. Because USDA does not assign an exact number of trees to an individual gift, we preserve it as a restoration contribution rather than estimate a tree count.

Why Chippewa comes first

Minnesota was Robert’s childhood home, so Chippewa National Forest remains the anchor of this living tribute. We prioritize exact-tree options that let contributors choose Chippewa directly; USDA remains only an optional official restoration-gift route.

Questions

Before you give

Does the memorial website handle my payment?

No. Every payment is completed on the selected provider’s own secure checkout. The Robert Dickinson Memorial never receives card numbers, CVVs, bank details, or payment credentials.

How are the tree totals counted?

After a successful payment, contributors return here and report the exact tree quantity shown by the provider. The memorial uses an honor system and reviews each report before adding it to the lifetime total.

What if a provider accepts a restoration donation but gives no exact tree number?

That gift is still preserved in Robert’s living tribute, but it is counted separately as a forest-restoration contribution rather than converted into a guessed number of trees.

Does choosing a forest always guarantee the exact planting site?

Not always. Some planting programs offer a preferred forest or state but may use another current restoration site when conditions or project availability change. The provider’s checkout and current planting terms are authoritative.
