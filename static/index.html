Scientific legacy

Science that changed how we see Earth

Rather than a single linear path, Bob’s work formed a connected scientific landscape. Foundational ideas in atmospheric dynamics, climate change, modeling, land–atmosphere exchange, observations from space, and the coupled Earth repeatedly converged as the scale of his questions widened.

  • Atmospheric dynamics
  • Climate change
  • Climate modeling
  • Land–atmosphere interactions
  • Observation from space
  • A coupled Earth
Other frontiers

He also made important contributions in many additional areas, including:

Planetary atmospheresTropical deforestationRegional climate modelingCarbon & nitrogen cyclesDynamic vegetationSolar geoengineeringHydrology & droughtAerosols & radiation

He opened new frontiers in planetary-atmosphere science

Robert advanced the theory of planetary waves and developed pioneering global models of upper-atmospheric dynamics and radiation, extending from Earth’s stratosphere and thermosphere to the atmospheres of Venus and Mars.

He established a quantitative foundation for understanding global warming

Robert’s seminal work on radiative forcing, climate sensitivity, and greenhouse gases helped establish how changes in Earth’s energy balance translate into global temperature change, a foundation for modern assessments of greenhouse warming and global change.

He pioneered integration of land processes into global climate models

Robert transformed the land process from a passive boundary into an active, interacting component of global climate models. He connected atmosphere, biosphere, and hydrosphere into a fuller and faithful picture of the Earth system.

He inspired generations of scientists

Robert passed on more than knowledge. His rigor, curiosity, breadth of vision, and generosity live on through generations of students, postdocs, collaborators, and scientific communities.
